The best Fantastical alternative depends on which part of Fantastical you are replacing.

If you use several calendar providers, tasks, scheduling links, travel time, and Apple devices, Fantastical may still be the sensible choice. If you only connected Google Calendar because you wanted a proper Mac app, a narrower product can cost less and fit the workflow better.

I build [hora Calendar](https://horacal.app), one of the options in this guide. That gives me a point of view, not a neutral lab coat. I have kept the comparison to facts the products publish themselves and made the tradeoffs explicit, including the cases where hora is the wrong choice.

Prices and plans change. The models below were checked against official product and pricing pages on July 14, 2026. Follow the linked pricing page before buying.

## The short answer

- **Stay with Fantastical** if you need several providers, tasks, scheduling tools, and a mature app across Apple devices and Windows.
- **Choose hora Calendar** if Google Calendar is your source of truth and you want a focused native Mac client with annual and one-time purchase options.
- **Choose BusyCal** if you want a highly configurable Mac calendar, broad provider support, and a perpetual license.
- **Choose Notion Calendar** if free access, Notion integration, scheduling links, and Google or iCloud calendars cover your needs.
- **Use Apple Calendar** if the app included with macOS already handles your accounts and you do not need a specialized Google workflow.
- **Choose Morgen** if tasks, time blocking, and cross-platform daily planning matter more than a traditional calendar client.

That is the decision in one minute. The rest of the guide explains what you give up with each switch.

## First, decide why you want to leave Fantastical

[Fantastical](https://flexibits.com/fantastical) is not simply a Google Calendar skin. It connects to Google, iCloud, Microsoft 365, Exchange, CalDAV, Todoist, and other services. Its current product includes natural-language event creation, tasks, multiple calendar views, calendar sets, travel time, scheduling links through Openings, meeting proposals, and conference integrations.

The [official Fantastical plan comparison](https://flexibits.com/pricing?product=fantastical) separates a free Essentials tier from Flexibits Premium. Premium becomes easier to justify as you use more of the suite.

Before replacing it, name the actual problem:

1. **Price:** you want a perpetual license, a cheaper annual plan, or a free app.
2. **Scope:** you use Google Calendar only and do not need the rest of the provider matrix.
3. **Platform:** you need Linux, Android, or a browser workflow Fantastical does not center.
4. **Planning style:** you want task-led time blocking or automatic planning, not only calendar management.
5. **Simplicity:** the broad feature set creates more interface than your day requires.

If you cannot identify one of those problems, switching may create work without improving the calendar.

## hora Calendar: for a Google-only Mac workflow

[hora Calendar](https://horacal.app) is the narrowest alternative in this guide. It is a native macOS client built specifically around Google Calendar.

That focus means Google remains the source of truth. hora connects to Google's API, supports multiple Google accounts, detects common video meeting links, keeps upcoming events in the menu bar and desktop widgets, and caches calendar data locally for the Mac experience. Quick Add handles natural-language event creation, while Focus Time finds open slots inside a range you choose and previews the blocks before creating them.

It does not connect to iCloud, Exchange, Microsoft 365, or arbitrary CalDAV servers. It also does not replace Fantastical Openings with a hosted booking marketplace. If either capability is essential, choose a broader app.

The current Mac App Store offer is **$29.99 per year or $49.90 for one-time access**, with Family Sharing on both options.

**Choose hora when:** Google Calendar is the only calendar system you need, Mac is where you plan, and a focused native workflow matters more than provider breadth.

**Do not choose hora when:** work requires Exchange or Microsoft 365, you need iCloud calendars in the same client, or you need the same app on Windows and mobile today.

For a closer look at the technical boundary, read [Google Calendar API vs CalDAV](/blog/2026-05-21-google-calendar-api-vs-caldav/). For the current product story, see [hora Calendar 1.0](/blog/2026-05-29-google-calendar-app-for-mac-hora-1-0/).

## BusyCal: for Mac users who want depth and a perpetual license

[BusyCal](https://www.busymac.com/busycal/) is the closest fit for someone who values a traditional, powerful Mac calendar but wants to leave a recurring subscription.

Its official feature set includes Day, Week, Month, Year Planner, and List views, calendar sets, smart filters, integrated tasks, natural-language input, a menu bar calendar, travel time, time zones, weather, and extensive appearance controls.

The [BusyCal store](https://www.busymac.com/store/) lists the Mac app at **$49.99**. It is a perpetual license, not a subscription. The purchase includes 18 months of updates. After that, the last covered version keeps working, while a discounted renewal is required for newer updates. BusyCal for iPhone, iPad, and Apple Watch is a separate $9.99 purchase.

That licensing detail matters. "One-time purchase" does not mean every future release is included forever. It means the licensed version does not stop working when the update window ends.

**Choose BusyCal when:** you want deep customization, multiple calendar providers, tasks, and a perpetual Mac license.

**Do not choose BusyCal when:** you want the narrowest possible Google-focused interface or expect every future update and mobile platform in one purchase.

## Notion Calendar: for a free calendar beside Notion work

[Notion Calendar](https://www.notion.com/product/calendar) is free and available on macOS, Windows, iPhone, and Android. Its official product page highlights menu bar meeting access, scheduling links, time-zone support, keyboard workflows, and Notion workspace integration.

Notion currently says Calendar integrates with Google Calendar accounts and Apple iCloud-synced calendars. It can show Notion database items beside calendar events, but it does not turn Google Calendar events into Notion database entries.

That makes it compelling if projects already live in Notion. It is less compelling as a replacement for Fantastical's broad provider support.

**Choose Notion Calendar when:** you want a free app, use Google or iCloud calendars, and benefit from seeing Notion work beside your schedule.

**Do not choose Notion Calendar when:** you need Exchange, Microsoft 365, CalDAV, travel-time features, or Fantastical's deeper calendar sets.

## Apple Calendar: test the app you already own

[Apple Calendar](https://support.apple.com/guide/calendar/welcome/mac) can manage events from several account types, including iCloud and Google. Apple's current macOS guide also documents invitations, recurring events, Focus filters, time zones, calendar subscriptions, and scheduled Reminders shown alongside events.

It is the correct baseline because it is already installed and costs nothing beyond the Mac. Before buying another app, add the accounts you actually use and spend a workday in it.

The tradeoff is specialization. Apple Calendar provides a broad system calendar, not a Google-specific client, task planner, or hosted scheduling service. Some provider-specific actions may still send you to the provider's website. Apple notes, for example, that creating or deleting a Google calendar itself may need to happen on google.com.

**Choose Apple Calendar when:** your needs are conventional and the built-in app already keeps your accounts coherent.

**Do not choose Apple Calendar when:** you are switching specifically for Google-first details, advanced scheduling, deep customization, or task-led planning.

## Morgen: for planning tasks across platforms

[Morgen](https://www.morgen.so/pricing) is a different kind of alternative. It combines calendars with task integrations, time blocking, calendar automations, booking links, and an AI planning layer. Its desktop app supports macOS, Windows, and Linux, with mobile and browser access as well.

The current individual plan is **$15 per month billed yearly or $30 billed monthly**, following a 14-day trial. Morgen lists Google Calendar, Outlook, iCloud through CalDAV, Fastmail, Notion, Todoist, Linear, ClickUp, Google Tasks, and other integrations.

That price buys a planning system, not merely a calendar window. Comparing it with hora or Apple Calendar on cost alone would miss the point.

**Choose Morgen when:** tasks come from several tools, you want time blocking and planning assistance, and you work across operating systems.

**Do not choose Morgen when:** Google Calendar already contains the plan and you only want a focused Mac client.

## When Fantastical is still the right choice

Stay with Fantastical if several of these are true:

- Your schedule spans Google, iCloud, Microsoft, Exchange, or CalDAV accounts.
- You use tasks beside calendar events.
- Calendar Sets are part of how you separate work and personal contexts.
- Travel time and time-to-leave alerts matter.
- Openings or Meeting Proposals replace a separate scheduling service.
- You want one established app across Mac, iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch, Vision Pro, and Windows.

Switching away from a product you actually use is not optimization. It is migration work.

## A ten-minute test before you migrate

Install the candidate and use your real accounts. Then complete these steps:

1. Add every provider you need, not only your easiest Google account.
2. Create an event with an invitee, conference link, reminder, and recurrence.
3. Move one occurrence and confirm the result in the provider's web app.
4. Respond to an invitation and check the response on another device.
5. Find the next meeting and join it without opening the main calendar window.
6. Disconnect the network and check what remains visible.
7. Recreate one workflow you rely on in Fantastical, such as a Calendar Set, task view, or scheduling link.

The first four steps test calendar fidelity. The last three test whether the replacement fits your day.

## Which Fantastical alternative should you pick?

Start with the system you cannot change.

If that system is several calendar providers, Fantastical or BusyCal remain the strongest fits in this group. If it is Notion, Notion Calendar removes a layer of context switching. If it is a cross-platform task stack, Morgen has the more relevant planning model. If your needs are simple, Apple Calendar may already be enough.

If your system is Google Calendar and your problem is the browser experience on Mac, hora is built around that exact constraint.
